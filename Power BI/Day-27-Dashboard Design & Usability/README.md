# Power BI — Day 27: Dashboard Design & Usability

**Level:** Beginner to Intermediate  
**Estimated time:** 75–120 minutes

## Goal
Bring together Days 22–26 skills to create a clear, business-focused report page that is easy to navigate and understand.

## Before you begin
1. Open your completed Day 26 PBIX.
2. Choose **File → Save As**.
3. Save a copy as `Day27_Dashboard_Design_Usability.pbix`.
4. Keep the previous day's file unchanged.

## Part A — Plan the layout
Use your existing sales report and organize it into clear sections:
- **Top:** report title and key KPI cards.
- **Middle:** primary analysis visuals, such as sales by category and monthly sales trend.
- **Bottom or separate page:** supporting detail, such as a region/category matrix or product table.
- **Filter area:** region and date/year slicers in a predictable location.
- **Navigation:** buttons with clear labels and consistent placement.

This is a suggestion, not a strict template. Keep visuals that answer useful business questions; remove or move redundant ones.

## Part B — Improve visual hierarchy
1. Make the report title clear and descriptive.
2. Make KPI cards easy to notice first.
3. Make primary analysis charts larger than supporting details where appropriate.
4. Keep titles, fonts, number formats, and spacing consistent.
5. Use a small color palette and reserve strong colors for meaningful emphasis.
6. Align visual edges and maintain consistent gaps.
7. Check readability at normal viewing size.

## Part C — Reduce clutter
Review each visual:
- Does it answer a business question?
- Is its title clear?
- Is the legend necessary?
- Are gridlines or borders helping?
- Are labels overlapping or too dense?
- Could it move to a detail page?

Remove unnecessary elements, but keep information needed to interpret the data correctly.

## Part D — Test usability and interactions
1. Test the Region and Year/Date slicers.
2. Clear slicers and confirm the full report returns.
3. Test your bookmark and button.
4. Test drill-down, drill-through, and Back navigation.
5. Hover over a data point to check tooltips.
6. Confirm visual interactions behave as intended.
7. Ensure viewers can return to the overview page.

## Part E — Business-focused review
Write three short observations supported by your actual report:
- Which category has the highest net sales?
- What trend do you observe over time?
- Which region, channel, or product deserves further investigation?

Do not guess or invent findings. If your visuals do not support a conclusion, state what additional analysis is needed.

## Key takeaway
A strong dashboard is not the page with the most visuals. It helps viewers find important information, understand it quickly, and explore further without confusion.
